Most developer portfolios optimize for the first three seconds of a visit and forget the next ten minutes. Once a reader clicks into an essay or architecture write-up, wide multi-column grids and flashy animations turn into friction.

Great editorial platforms like **note.com** solve this with almost deceptive simplicity: a quiet canvas, generous vertical rhythm, and a focused reading column around **640 pixels** wide.

## Why 640px works so well

At `17px` body text, a `640px` column yields roughly **65 to 75 characters per line**. That is the sweet spot where the eye sweeps from the end of one line to the start of the next without losing its place or tiring over long technical paragraphs.

<Callout label="Design Principle">
  Keep structural chrome razor-sharp on landing pages, then get completely out of the reader's way inside long-form prose.
</Callout>

Three small rules carry most of the weight:

1. **Line height around 1.85**: Tight leading feels dense in technical prose; generous leading creates breathing room around inline code and links.
2. **One purposeful accent color**: Using emerald (`#41c9b4`) exclusively for active states, subtle markers, and blockquotes preserves calm visual hierarchy.
3. **Stable background continuity**: Rather than shifting background colors between sections, hairline `1px` borders mark transitions without jarring the eye.

## Sharp structure meets quiet prose

Borrowing the crisp geometry, tabular precision, and monospace metadata from engineering sites like `withspecific.com` gives the portfolio authority at a glance. Pairing that structure with a warm charcoal dark mode and clean `note.com` reading ergonomics ensures the writing actually gets read.

> Good interface craft is invisible while you are reading—you only notice how effortlessly you reached the final paragraph.

Next time you redesign a personal site, try opening your longest post and reading it end to end on both light and dark surfaces. Any distraction you feel at minute four should be deleted.
